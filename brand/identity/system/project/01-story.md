# Story

## The name

| | |
|---|---|
| भू · *bhoo* | the Earth, the ground |
| गोल · *gol* | round, a sphere |
| भूगोल · *bhoogol* | the round Earth, and the everyday Hindi word for geography |

Write it **Bhoogol** in sentences and **bhoogol** in the logo. Say *BHOO-gol*; "boo-goal" is close enough. In Devanagari it is always भूगोल.

## Where it comes from

The word is old. Classical Indian astronomy used *bhūgola* for the Earth-sphere. Aryabhata's *Āryabhaṭīya* (499 CE) has a whole chapter on the sphere, the *Golapāda*, and argues that the Earth turns on its axis. Fifteen centuries later, every Indian child meets the same word on a school timetable, usually beside a dusty desk globe and an outline map.

Bhoogol takes that word back to its literal meaning: a round Earth you can pick up and spin.

## Three memories the brand draws on

1. **The Blue Marble.** In 1972 the Apollo 17 crew photographed the whole Earth, lit from behind them: blue sea, brown and green land, white cloud. It is the reason a world in this brand is a blue disc with soft land on it.
2. **Kanche.** These are the glass marbles kids flick across the dust in every Indian gali. A marble is a little world you can hold. In the product, marbles are what you collect.
3. **"Mark the following on the outline map."** This is the geography-exam instruction everyone remembers. The hand-drawn loop, tick and arrow, our *teacher's marks*, come from that pencil, now in marigold.

## The promise

**See the whole world, and remember it.** The globe is real and the data is real. The play is what makes it stick.

## Who it is for

- **Students** from about 13 up, revising or just curious.
- **Teachers**, who put it on a classroom screen.
- **Adults** who like knowing where things are.

It is rooted in India and made for everyone. The identity should feel at home in a Pune classroom and a Lisbon living room.
